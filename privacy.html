Orbit Privacy Policy

Last updated November 17, 2026

Orbit is a personal productivity and creator dashboard made by Jake Sperry for his own use. It runs as a Mac app and a web app. This page explains what data Orbit uses, where it is kept, and how to remove it.

Google and YouTube data

When you connect a YouTube account, Orbit asks Google for permission to:

  • Upload videos (youtube.upload) — to post Shorts you create or approve in Orbit to your own channel.
  • Read your YouTube account (youtube.readonly) — to show your channel name, subscriber and view counts, your recent uploads, and to search public videos.
  • Read YouTube Analytics (yt-analytics.readonly, yt-analytics-monetary.readonly) — to show daily views, watch time, subscribers and estimated earnings on your own dashboard.

Orbit uses this data only to show it back to you and to do the actions you ask for. It does not sell, rent or share Google user data, does not use it for advertising, and does not use it to train AI models. Orbit’s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

Where your data is stored

  • Google sign-in tokens are stored only on your Mac, encrypted with the macOS Keychain. They are never sent to any server other than Google’s.
  • Summary numbers (for example daily views) and your Orbit content (tasks, notes, bot drafts) are synced to your own private Orbit account so your phone and Mac match. Only you can sign in to that account.
  • Other services you connect yourself (such as Vexora AI, Stripe or ESPN) are only contacted from your devices, with your own login.

Removing access and data

You can disconnect YouTube in Orbit (AI Hub → Connections) at any time, and revoke Orbit’s access in your Google Account at myaccount.google.com/permissions. Disconnecting deletes the stored tokens from your Mac. To delete synced data, sign out of Orbit and ask for deletion by email.
